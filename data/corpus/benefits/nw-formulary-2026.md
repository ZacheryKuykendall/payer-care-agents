## How tiers work

Every covered drug sits on one of five tiers. Tier placement drives the member's cost
share, which is defined in each plan's Summary of Benefits rather than here. A drug
may also carry one or more utilization management flags that apply before cost share
is calculated.

## Utilization management flags

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| PA | Prior authorization required before the pharmacy claim will pay |
| ST | Step therapy: the member must try a preferred alternative first |
| QL | Quantity limit per fill or per period |
| SP | Specialty pharmacy dispensing required |
| LA | Limited access through designated pharmacies only |

## Step therapy

Where a step therapy flag applies, the plan requires a documented trial of at least
one preferred alternative within the prior 180 days, or documentation of
contraindication, intolerance, or treatment failure. A pharmacy claim rejection with
reject code 75 indicates a step therapy edit.

Step therapy requirements do not apply to members continuing therapy that was stable
at the time of enrollment. Continuity of care protection lasts for the first 90 days
of the plan year.

## Quantity limits

Quantity limits are expressed per 30-day supply unless noted. A prescriber may
request a quantity limit exception using the coverage determination process. The plan
has 72 hours for a standard coverage determination and 24 hours for an expedited
request once all required clinical information is received.

## Transition fill

New members and continuing members whose drug moves to a non-covered status receive a
one-time 30-day transition fill during the first 90 days of the plan year. The plan
sends a written transition notice within three business days of the transition fill.

## Formulary exceptions

A prescriber may request a formulary exception by submitting a supporting statement
that the preferred alternatives would not be as effective, would cause an adverse
effect, or both. Approved exceptions are honored through the end of the plan year.
