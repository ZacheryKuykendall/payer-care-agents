## Appeal versus grievance

An appeal challenges a coverage decision: a denied service, a denied payment, or a
reduction or early termination of a service the member is receiving. A grievance is a
complaint about anything else, including quality of care, wait times, or how the
member was treated.

A member who is unsure which one they want is always routed to a human. The automated
assistant in this architecture never files, withdraws, or characterizes an appeal on
a member's behalf.

## Filing timeframes

| Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- |
| File a standard appeal | Within 65 calendar days of the denial notice |
| File a grievance | Within 60 calendar days of the event |
| Plan decision, standard pre-service appeal | 30 calendar days |
| Plan decision, expedited pre-service appeal | 72 hours |
| Plan decision, payment appeal | 60 calendar days |
| Grievance resolution | 30 calendar days, extendable by 14 days |

## Who may file

The member, the member's appointed representative with a completed appointment of
representative form, or the treating provider acting on the member's behalf for a
pre-service appeal.

## Independent review

If the plan upholds a denial on a standard pre-service appeal, the case is
automatically forwarded to an independent review entity. The member does not need to
take any further action for that forwarding to occur.

## What the assistant may tell a member

The assistant may describe these timeframes, explain the difference between an appeal
and a grievance, and confirm where an existing appeal stands. It may not advise on
the merits of an appeal, draft appeal language, or predict an outcome. Requests of
that kind route to the appeals and grievances queue.
